A handover clue hiding in the VG-4 completion count

What counts as a completed move? At handover, a vacuum-handling percentage is useful only when the receiving shift knows which part of the movement it actually describes. VG-4 uses complete pick-transfer-place success as its commissioning measure for a locked recipe.

Following the workpiece

The workpiece must travel without interruption and finish inside the specified placement tolerance. Every initiated attempt belongs in the population used to calculate the percentage. An aborted attempt is still a started attempt for this purpose, as is a transit drop. Completion is a sequence-level measure. In handover discussions, the definition directs attention to the finished handling task rather than to an isolated movement that does not establish placement within the tolerance specified by the recipe.

Reading the clearance

The reported VG-4 completion rate sits 4 percentage points above the comparison reference. It is not strictly below. Here, percentage points express the arithmetic difference between a reported rate and its reference, rather than a relative percentage increase over that reference. Keep the direction with the difference. An unsigned gap alone would not say whether the completion rate cleared the reference or fell short of it.

What travels with the figure

The applicable session contains 50–75 trials conducted under the same commissioning recipe throughout. Keep the rigid workpiece set and its starting-pose distribution attached to the handover description. Vacuum setting, movement speed and transfer path also belong to that operating context. Final placement tolerance supplies the finish criterion, rather than an informal judgment that a part arrived. Before the next shift uses the comparison, check that these recipe details still describe its planned work.
